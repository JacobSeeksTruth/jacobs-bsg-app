Alternative Translations ▾

Different Bible translations add crucial context missed in English. Wherever available, I’ve included original Hebrew, Greek, or Aramaic (Peshitta) insights to capture these deeper meanings. When a single word carries so much cultural, emotional, or situational weight that it requires a sentence or an entire paragraph to explain in another language, linguists and translators describe this with terms such as “untranslatable,” “semantic range,” or “loaded term.” Look for the “Untranslatable Word” dropdowns for those cases.

Hebrew ▾
Genesis 1:1 ▾
בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ
Bereshit bara Elohim et hashamayim ve-et ha-aretz

Literal: “In beginning created God the heavens and the earth.”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • בְּרֵאשִׁית (bereshit) – in beginning
  • בָּרָא (bara) – he created (unique divine creation verb)
  • אֱלֹהִים (Elohim) – God (plural form, singular meaning)
  • אֵת (et) – direct-object marker (untranslated)
  • הַשָּׁמַיִם (hashamayim) – the heavens
  • וְאֵת (ve-et) – and + object marker
  • הָאָרֶץ (ha-aretz) – the earth
Untranslatable Word ▾

אֱלֹהִים (Elohim) – Plural in form yet almost always takes singular verbs when referring to the one true God. It can convey majesty, intensity, or the fullness of divine power rather than numerical plurality. English “God” is the closest single-word equivalent but loses the grammatical and theological nuance of the Hebrew plural-of-majesty.

Proverbs 3:5 ▾
בְּטַח אֶל־יְהוָה בְּכָל־לִבֶּךָ וְאֶל־בִּינָתְךָ אַל־תִּשָּׁעֵן
Betach el-YHWH bechol-libbecha ve-el-binatecha al-tishaen

Word Breakdown ▾

  • אֶל־יְהוָה (el-YHWH) – to/in Yahweh
  • בְּכָל־לִבֶּךָ (bechol-libbecha) – with all your heart
  • וְאֶל־בִּינָתְךָ (ve-el-binatecha) – and on your understanding
  • אַל־תִּשָּׁעֵן (al-tishaen) – do not lean / rely
Psalm 23:1 ▾
יְהוָה רֹעִי לֹא אֶחְסָר
YHWH ro’i lo echsar

Literal: “Yahweh is my shepherd; I shall not lack.”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • יְהוָה (YHWH) – the personal name of God
  • רֹעִי (ro’i) – my shepherd
  • לֹא אֶחְסָר (lo echsar) – I shall not lack / want
Untranslatable Word ▾

רֹעִי (ro’i) – “My shepherd.” In ancient Near-Eastern culture a shepherd was far more than a caretaker of animals: he was protector, provider, guide, and sometimes king-image. The single Hebrew word carries relational intimacy and total dependence that English “shepherd” only partially conveys.

Greek ▾
John 3:16 ▾
Οὕτως γὰρ ἠγάπησεν ὁ Θεὸς τὸν κόσμον, ὥστε τὸν Υἱὸν τὸν μονογενῆ ἔδωκεν, ἵνα πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων εἰς αὐτὸν μὴ ἀπόληται ἀλλ’ ἔχῃ ζωὴν αἰώνιον.
Houtōs gar ēgapēsen ho Theos ton kosmon, hōste ton Huion ton monogenē edōken, hina pas ho pisteuōn eis auton mē apolētai all’ echē zōēn aiōnion.

Literal: “For thus God loved the world, that He gave the only-begotten Son, so that everyone believing in Him should not perish but have eternal life.”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • Οὕτως γὰρ – For thus / in this way
  • ἠγάπησεν (ēgapēsen) – he loved (aorist of agapaō)
  • ὁ Θεὸς – God
  • τὸν κόσμον – the world
  • τὸν Υἱὸν τὸν μονογενῆ – the Son, the only-begotten / unique
  • ἔδωκεν – he gave
  • πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων – everyone who believes
  • ζωὴν αἰώνιον – life eternal / age-lasting
Untranslatable Word ▾

ἠγάπησεν / ἀγάπη (agapaō / agapē) – Often rendered simply “loved,” yet it denotes a deliberate, self-giving, covenantal love that seeks the highest good of the other, independent of worthiness. English “love” covers too broad a range (friendship, romance, preference) to capture the focused, sacrificial force of the Greek term here.

μονογενῆ (monogenē) – “Only-begotten” or “unique/one-of-a-kind.” It stresses uniqueness of relationship and nature rather than merely “only child.” English struggles to convey both the singularity and the relational intimacy in one word.

Romans 3:23 ▾
πάντες γὰρ ἥμαρτον καὶ ὑστεροῦνται τῆς δόξης τοῦ Θεοῦ
Pantes gar hēmarton kai hysterountai tēs doxēs tou Theou

Literal: “For all sinned and fall short of the glory of God”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • πάντες – all
  • ἥμαρτον – sinned (aorist)
  • ὑστεροῦνται – fall short / lack
  • τῆς δόξης – of the glory
  • τοῦ Θεοῦ – of God
Romans 6:23 ▾
τὰ γὰρ ὀψώνια τῆς ἁμαρτίας θάνατος, τὸ δὲ χάρισμα τοῦ Θεοῦ ζωὴ αἰώνιος ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ τῷ Κυρίῳ ἡμῶν.
Ta gar opsōnia tēs hamartias thanatos, to de charisma tou Theou zōē aiōnios en Christō Iēsou tō Kyriō hēmōn.

Literal: “For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • ὀψώνια (opsōnia) – wages / soldier’s pay
  • ἁμαρτίας – of sin
  • θάνατος – death
  • χάρισμα (charisma) – free gift / grace-gift
  • ζωὴ αἰώνιος – eternal life
Untranslatable Word ▾

χάρισμα (charisma) – A gift that flows from charis (grace). It is not earned wages but an undeserved bestowal. English “gift” is accurate yet often feels too ordinary; the Greek term underscores the gracious, free character of what God gives in contrast to the earned “wages” of sin.

Walking in the Love of God Through Faithful Obedience

Congratulations on finishing every verse! These verses form a beautiful path of faith.

Devotional

In the beginning God created all things (Genesis 1:1). This same God so loved the world that He gave His only Son so that whoever believes in Him will have eternal life (John 3:16).

We have all sinned and fall short of God’s glory (Romans 3:23). The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus (Romans 6:23).

Trust in the Lord with all your heart (Proverbs 3:5). Seek first His kingdom and righteousness (Matthew 6:33). The Lord is your Shepherd; you shall not want (Psalm 23:1).

You can do all things through Christ who strengthens you (Philippians 4:13). And we know that for those who love God, all things work together for good (Romans 8:28).

Receive His love. Follow Jesus in trusting obedience. He will lead, strengthen, and guide you every step of the way.
