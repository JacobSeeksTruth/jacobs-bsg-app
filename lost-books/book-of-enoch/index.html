About This App

The Book of Enoch isn't just one single text—it’s actually a collection of three distinct books plus the Book of Giants. To get the full picture, read them in this order: 1 Enoch, the Book of Giants, 2 Enoch, and 3 Enoch.

The best place to pause your Bible reading and dive into these texts is right between Genesis 5:24 and Genesis 6:8, just before the Great Flood begins. In Genesis, Enoch’s entire life is covered in just four brief verses (Genesis 5:21–24), followed immediately by the mysterious mention of the "sons of God" and the Nephilim in Genesis 6:1–4. These four Enochic books zoom in on that exact window of time to fill in the blanks.

How to Read Them Alongside Genesis

Genesis 5:21–24 (Enoch Walks with God):
Genesis simply states that Enoch lived 365 years, walked faithfully with God, and then "was no more, because God took him away."

  • Read 2 Enoch (The Book of Secrets) here: This picks up at the exact moment God takes Enoch, detailing his journey through the ten heavens, his brief return to Earth to instruct his sons, and his final assumption into heaven.
  • Read 3 Enoch (The Book of Palaces) here: Though framed as a much later vision by Rabbi Ishmael, the backstory it tells happens right at this point in Genesis—explaining Enoch's transformation into the supreme archangel Metatron.

Genesis 6:1–4 (The Fallen Angels and the Nephilim):
Genesis briefly mentions the "sons of God" having children with human women, producing the mighty Nephilim (giants) as human wickedness begins to spiral out of control.

  • Read 1 Enoch (The Book of Enoch) here: Specifically, the opening section (The Book of the Watchers) dramatically expands on these four verses, telling the full story of the 200 fallen angels, the forbidden knowledge they taught humanity, and Enoch acting as an intermediary between them and God.
  • Read The Book of Giants here: Fitting directly alongside 1 Enoch, this text shifts the perspective to the Nephilim themselves as they are plagued by nightmares and realize their violent reign is about to be ended by God's judgment.

Genesis 6:5–8 (The Coming of the Flood):
At this point in Genesis, God sees how corrupt the earth has become and decides to wipe it clean, sparing only Noah.

  • Return to Genesis here: All four Enochic texts build up to this exact moment. Once you finish them, resuming Genesis at verse 6:5 seamlessly transitions you into the story of Noah and the Ark.

Book of Enoch

The Book of Enoch (also called 1 Enoch) is an ancient Jewish apocalyptic text traditionally ascribed to Enoch, the great-grandfather of Noah. Most scholars date the older sections (especially the Book of the Watchers) to approximately 300–200 BC, while the Book of Parables is generally placed around the 1st century BC. It is not included in the Jewish or most Christian canons, but it is regarded as scripture by the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church.

The book expands on Genesis 6 and contains unique material about the fall of the Watchers (angels), the origin of evil spirits and the Nephilim, the reasons for the Flood, heavenly journeys, astronomical secrets, dream visions, and the coming judgment and messianic kingdom.

BOOK OF THE WATCHERS
0:00 0:00

Chapters 1–36. This is the oldest major section. It opens with Enoch’s blessing and a vision of the coming judgment.

The core narrative describes the fall of the Watchers (200 angels led by Semjaza and Azazel) who descend to earth, take human wives, and teach forbidden knowledge (weapon-making, cosmetics, astrology, etc.). Their offspring, the giants (Nephilim), bring violence and corruption.

The archangels report the chaos to God, who decrees judgment: the Flood will cleanse the earth, the Watchers will be bound, and the spirits of the giants will become evil spirits that afflict humanity. Enoch is taken on journeys through the cosmos and shown the places of punishment and blessing.

BOOK OF PARABLES
0:00 0:00

Chapters 37–71. This section contains three parables (similitudes) focused on the coming judgment, the Son of Man / Elect One, and the vindication of the righteous.

It develops a strong messianic figure who is pre-existent, sits on the throne of glory, judges the kings and the mighty, and dwells with the righteous. Themes include the resurrection, the transformation of heaven and earth, and the final defeat of evil.

Many scholars see close parallels with later New Testament “Son of Man” language.

ASTRONOMICAL BOOK
0:00 0:00

Chapters 72–82. Also called the Book of the Heavenly Luminaries. The angel Uriel reveals to Enoch the laws of the sun, moon, stars, and calendar.

It strongly defends a 364-day solar calendar and criticizes those who follow a lunar calendar. Detailed descriptions of the gates of heaven, the winds, the seasons, and the movement of the luminaries are given.

The section ends with Enoch’s predictions about the moral decline of later generations.

BOOK OF DREAM VISIONS
0:00 0:00

Chapters 83–90. Contains two dream visions. The first is a short vision of the Flood.

The second is the famous “Animal Apocalypse,” an allegorical history of the world from Adam to the Maccabean period (and beyond) in which humans are represented as animals and angels as stars or men.

It culminates in the judgment of the fallen stars, the building of a new house (temple), and the appearance of a white bull (messianic figure) who transforms all the animals.

EPISTLE OF ENOCH
0:00 0:00

Chapters 91–108. This final section includes the Apocalypse of Weeks (a schematic history divided into ten “weeks”), exhortations to the righteous, woes against sinners, teachings on the two ways, and additional material about the birth of Noah and future judgment.

It emphasizes moral instruction, the certainty of divine justice, and the ultimate blessing of the righteous.
